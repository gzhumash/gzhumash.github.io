Project 2 - Stage 1

Topic: Gym Exercises

When starting out at the gym, it can be overwhelming for some to figure out what exercises work what muscle groups. Strength training is one of the most effective things a person can do for their health, but it only works if it is done with a plan. Without knowing which muscle a given exercise targets and how to progress that exercise over time, people end up wasting time in the gym or training in a way that does not move them toward their goals. This project addresses that gap by giving users a simple way to look up exercises based on the exact muscle they want to train.

I chose this topic because it is one of the things I am most passionate about in my life. I work out every day and I love giving other people fitness advice. Because I already have a strong background in this area, it will make coming up with accurate content, deciding how it should be organized, and making sure that all the information is correct really easy.

There are already many fitness apps on the market, but most of them try to do too much at once. They combine workout tracking, meal plans, macro counters, and social feeds into a single cluttered experience, which makes it harder to find a simple answer to a simple question. My web app is intentionally narrow in scope. Instead of trying to be a full fitness platform, it directly and simply answers one question: what exercise targets what muscle, and how should it be performed. That focus on clarity is what will set this project apart from existing solutions.

App Description

Page 1: Landing / Home Page

The home page introduces the topic to first time visitors and directs them into the rest of the site. It will contain:

  • Hero Banner: A clear title and a one to two sentence statement explaining what the site does.
  • The "Why" and Problem Statement: A short introduction explaining why structured strength training matters, why targeting specific muscles is key to making progress, and how this app cuts through information overload.
  • About the Creator: A brief section with a picture of me in the gym (not confirmed, it might be just text) and two to three sentences on why I built this site and my personal experience with lifting. This will be my credentials and authority.
  • Call to Action: Buttons that send visitors directly to Page 2 ("Find Your Exercises") or Page 3 ("Learn Training Fundamentals").

Page 2: Find Your Exercises

The user first selects a major muscle group, then selects a specific muscle within that group, and finally sees the exercise data for that exact choice. The muscle groups and subgroups are listed below.

  • Chest
    • Upper Chest
    • Mid Chest
    • Inner Chest
    • Lower Chest
  • Back
    • Lats
    • Upper Back / Rhomboids
    • Lower Back / Erectors
    • Traps
  • Arms
    • Biceps
    • Triceps
    • Forearms
    • Brachialis
  • Shoulders
    • Front Delts
    • Rear Delts
    • Side Delts
    • Rotator Cuff
  • Legs
    • Quads
    • Glutes
    • Hamstrings
    • Calves

Once a specific muscle is chosen, the user will see a list of exercises that target it. Each muscle will have 1 to 4 applicable exercises. For every exercise, the app will show the exercise name, a short one to three sentence description of proper form, and the equipment or machine required to perform it. This exercise data will first be stored in a JSON file, and later moved into a database as the project develops.

Page 3: Learn Training Fundamentals

This page will explain the foundations of progressive overload and the main training variables, such as sets, reps, weight, and rest. Where Page 2 tells the user which exercises target which muscles, this page explains how to actually use those exercises over time to keep making progress. It will also include some basic plans, routines, and splits to follow.

Page 4: Contact Us

This page will let visitors sign up to receive personalized training guidance from me directly.

Outside Sources

  • M&S Exercise Video Database - a video library of exercises organized by body part, used as a reference for exercise names, form cues, and equipment.
  • NASM: Progressive Overload Explained - an overview of how to apply progressive overload, which will inform the content on Page 3.
